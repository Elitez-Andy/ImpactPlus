About Elitez

Elitez Group of Companies is a regional human resources firm founded in Singapore in 2010, providing full-suite HR solutions — recruitment and manpower outsourcing, business process outsourcing, HR administration, outplacement and learning & development — to clients across healthcare, IT, logistics, FMCG, security and government services. The Group combines local expertise with regional reach to serve clients throughout Southeast Asia, guided by its mission to "fulfil human potential without borders."

Elitez Pte Ltd, the Group's primary operating subsidiary, employs 150 people across 7 office units in Singapore. The company frames its responsibilities around three commitments it calls the 3Ps — People, Planet and Profit — recognising that as a service-based business, its greatest sustainability lever is the wellbeing and conduct of its own workforce, alongside the operational footprint of its offices.

Stakeholder relationships

The company organises its stakeholder relationships into three categories: contractual relationships with shareholders, clients and partners; support relationships with employees and suppliers; and influencing relationships with government, media and the public. This structure underpins how ESG priorities are weighed against day-to-day business decisions.

PART 2

Environmental performance

Total greenhouse gas emissions for the period were 57,689.83 kg CO₂e, an increase of 8.7% on the prior year, comprising Scope 2 (purchased electricity, 72.8%) and Scope 3 (water consumption and business air travel, 27.2%). Elitez does not own transport or non-transport assets and therefore reports no Scope 1 emissions. This is the third consecutive year emissions have been measured, giving the company a genuine multi-year baseline rather than a single snapshot.

Three-year trend

The sharp rise in Scope 3 emissions between AY21/22 and AY22/23 (+2,360%) reflects the resumption of business air travel following pandemic-related restrictions. Growth has since moderated: Scope 2 rose 5.6% and Scope 3 rose 18.4% in the most recent year, suggesting the initial rebound is stabilising into steadier, more manageable growth.

Path to target & recommendations

Measured against the AY21/22 baseline, Scope 2 emissions would need to fall to approximately 14,131 kg CO₂e to meet the 2030 target (-50%) — a further 66% reduction from the current 41,971 kg. The single highest-leverage lever available is the 100% renewable electricity commitment; achieving it would address the majority of this gap directly.

A caution on the Scope 3 target: the AY21/22 baseline (1,040 kg CO₂e) was recorded during pandemic-era travel restrictions and does not reflect typical operating conditions. Measured against it, the current -20% target would require Scope 3 to fall to roughly 832 kg — over 18x below the current AY23/24 figure of 15,710 kg. This target is likely no longer realistic as set, and should be recalibrated against a representative post-pandemic baseline year before being used to track progress.

Recommended next actions: (1) re-baseline the Scope 3 target against AY22/23 or AY23/24 rather than AY21/22; (2) prioritise the supplier engagement programme in Part 5, since value-chain travel and facilities are the largest addressable categories; (3) continue the emissions-avoidance initiatives below, which are already estimated to offset roughly 16% of the annual footprint.

Climate-related risk

Physical risks, such as extreme weather disrupting office operations, are managed through business continuity plans that include remote-working provisions. Transition risks — principally rising compliance costs from carbon-related regulation, and reputational exposure if ESG expectations are not met — are monitored on an ongoing basis and addressed through the certifications and disclosures set out in this report.

Scope 3 emissions remain the hardest category to monitor across the industry: there is no strict regulatory requirement in Singapore mandating full value-chain disclosure, and most companies concentrate reduction efforts on Scope 1 and 2, where data is easier to collect. Elitez's decision to track water consumption and business air travel under Scope 3 already puts it ahead of many peers on this front — though, as above, genuine improvement will depend on extending that visibility further into the supply chain.
